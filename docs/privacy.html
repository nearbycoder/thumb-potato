Privacy

Privacy Policy

Thumb Potato has no accounts and no servers of its own. Your backlog, ratings and play sessions stay on your device and, on Apple platforms, in your own iCloud. There is nothing for us to collect, so we don't.

Effective November 3, 2026

The short version

  • No accounts, no servers. The app never signs you in and never talks to a server we run.
  • Your data stays yours. Apple devices sync through your own iCloud; Android keeps everything on the device.
  • No analytics or tracking. No ads, no telemetry, no third-party SDKs.
  • Game lookups go directly to the source. Wikipedia, Wikidata, Steam, CheapShark and GitHub Pages see the requests, not us. RAWG too, but only if you add your own key.

What the app stores

Thumb Potato keeps only what it needs to work: the games in your backlog and their status (Want to Play, Playing, Beaten, Shelved), the platform you play each one on, hours played and progress, whether you own it, your star ratings and notes, the games you follow for release alerts, planned play sessions, the platforms you say you own, your region and app settings. If you choose to add a RAWG API key, that key is stored alongside your settings. A copy of the public game catalog is cached on the device so the app works quickly and offline.

Where it is stored

iPhone, iPad and Mac. Your data lives on the device and, when iCloud is enabled, in your own iCloud account using CloudKit's private database and the iCloud key-value store. Only you and your signed-in devices can read it. Apple hosts that data under its own privacy policy; we have no access to it and no way to see it.

Android. Your data is stored on the device only. The app can export it to a backup file (thumbpotato.backup JSON) and import that file again, on Android or on Apple devices. You decide where that file goes and who can see it; the app never uploads it anywhere.

Game data and artwork

To show games, box art, release dates and prices, the app fetches public data from third-party services. These requests go directly from your device to the service; nothing passes through us.

  • GitHub Pages, which hosts this website and the weekly game catalog the app downloads. See the GitHub privacy statement.
  • Wikipedia and Wikidata, for game descriptions, box art, search and release details. See the Wikimedia privacy policy.
  • Steam, for PC and Mac artwork, top sellers, reviews and Steam Deck status. See Valve's privacy policy.
  • CheapShark, for PC prices and deals. When you open a deal, CheapShark redirects you to the store. See the CheapShark privacy policy.
  • RAWG, only if you add your own API key. Requests to RAWG include that key and the games you search for or open. See the RAWG privacy policy.

Like any web request, these services can see your device's IP address and the games you look up. Price requests include the region you choose so prices show in your currency. The app sends nothing else: no identifiers, no contacts, no location.

Store links in Where to Play open the store's own website or app, where that store's privacy policy applies.

Optional permissions

  • Calendar. If you choose to add a play session to your calendar, the app asks for calendar access and uses it only to create and update those events. It does not read or scan your other events.
  • Notifications. If you turn on release-day alerts or session reminders, the app schedules local notifications on your device. No notification data leaves the device.

Both permissions are optional. The app works without them, and you can revoke them at any time in your system settings.

Analytics and tracking

None. Thumb Potato contains no analytics, crash reporting, advertising or tracking code, and it does not fingerprint your device. We cannot see how you use the app, and we would not want to. This website doesn't use cookies or analytics either.

Deleting your data

Deleting the app removes everything it stored on the device. On Apple platforms, data already synced to iCloud can be removed from Settings → Your name → iCloud → Manage Storage → Thumb Potato on iPhone and iPad, or System Settings → Apple Account → iCloud → Manage on a Mac. On Android, uninstalling the app or clearing its storage removes its data; delete any backup files you exported yourself.

Children

The app does not collect personal information from anyone, including children.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the new version will be published on this page with an updated effective date. Because the app has no servers, a change cannot affect data the app has already stored on your devices or in your iCloud.

Questions

Thumb Potato is open source, so you can read exactly what the app does in its source code. For questions about privacy, open an issue on GitHub.
